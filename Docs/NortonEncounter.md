# The research-station wreck and the lightless heads — 0.13.0

The burning car stands outside the research station, east of its front rooms. In 0.11.1 the entire encounter moved from the ranger cabin: car/fire/smoke, casualty/blood, letter and shotgun share the relocated layout coordinates. The vegetation clearing and navigation are regenerated around the new site. A clothed human casualty lies face up beside the car, with a torn abdominal opening, exposed coiled intestines and blood. The letter and double-barrel shotgun are separate visible interaction targets; searching the casualty also reads the letter. Existing persistent IDs retain earlier discovery and ammunition progress.

The letter to Mr. Norton is written separately in Hebrew and English and follows the selected language. It describes dead heads crawling on six legs, asks the hunter to stop them before they reach the houses, and warns that light will not repel them. Reading unlocks the threat. The introduction waits until the player closes the journal, then plays its own cry and creates one reachable enemy. Rereading the letter does not replay the introduction.

The new species follows a complete navigation path toward the current player position, including inside either amber-lit cabin and the weak-lit research station. Light and shelter checks do not repel it or prevent its damage. It has a six-legged scuttle, jaw-driven close bite and a 1.6-second mouth-opening spit with a glowing warning. The original two-slot encounter pool is restored in 0.13.0. The first introduction schedules another head after 48 active seconds; subsequent successful spawns use 42–60 seconds, even while a head is alive. Loading an introduced encounter waits 18 seconds. Occupied slots retry after five seconds. A burning body keeps its slot until complete disappearance, so its collapse/fire never gets overwritten. Every placement must have physical clearance and a complete path to the player.

The 0.12.0 Blender revision keeps the CC0 anatomical skin UVs and pore/roughness maps, with sunken sockets, wasted asymmetric cheeks, torn lip rims, 24 pointed crooked teeth, raised leg tendons and hooked claws. Dedicated Cinder materials add bruised mottling, fine veins and wet tissue. A quiet original twelve-second dread score starts within 24 metres, follows the closest living head at low gain and remains engaged until the last active head dies or retires; menu audio pauses with gameplay.

Lethal damage disables its hit collider. The approach score stops when no living heads remain. The imported 3.2-second death clip folds all six legs and rolls the head onto its cheek, with grounded feet and a settling twitch. Flames start after the collapse, at 3.2 seconds; orange light, crackling, smoke and embers accompany progressive char and ash dissolution. At 7.5 seconds the remains, light, particles and audio deactivate. Cleanup preserves the running appearance timer. Resetting a pooled head restores opaque skin, shadows, collider and animation. Zero time and menus freeze collapse, fire and recurrence.

A shotgun blast kills a head within an aimed, unobstructed 18-metre cone. Two axe contacts kill it. The shotgun also kills undead wolves; the original Walker retains its existing combat rules. The player holds the shotgun in the right hand and keeps the separate flashlight in the left hand through recoil and reload. The gun begins with two loaded cartridges and twelve spare. Three boxes of twelve cartridges are placed in the buildings. The two-barrel reload takes 1.7 seconds and consumes reserve ammunition when it finishes. Menus freeze reload, enemies, projectiles and fire effects.

Fireballs travel at 8.5 metres per second. A swept sphere collision catches both walls and the player, and each projectile can damage the player once. The first fireball starts a ten-second burn recovery timer, independent of ordinary bite injuries and shelter recovery. A second fireball during that timer is fatal. After ten seconds of active play, the next fireball counts as a fresh first hit. The HUD shows remaining recovery time. Close fatal head attacks use the existing fallen-camera/blood presentation with the actual head as attacker; distant fatal fire impacts show the death menu without moving the Walker into the scene.

Controls: **mouse wheel** cycles owned weapons; **TAB** opens/closes the pack with separate axe/shotgun selection buttons (missing weapons are disabled). **1** axe, **2** shotgun (number row or numpad), **left mouse** attack, **T** shotgun reload, **R or B** flashlight battery with either weapon. A weapon selection made during a swing/reload is applied when that action finishes. Battery replacement also works during a gun reload and does not change ammunition. Existing flashlight/boost, map, journal and fullscreen controls remain available. Checkpoint schema 5 retains discovery, the one-time introduction, shotgun, selected weapon, loaded/spare ammunition, pickup quantities and remaining burn recovery. Versions 1–4 migrate with their existing forest progress intact.

The updated Unity captures **50–52** show the live head and mouth, **64–67** show collapse, delayed ignition, ash erosion and cleared ground, **68** shows skin/mouth detail, and **69–71** show Walker retreat and return. Focused head authoring uses `Tools/Run-BlenderStep.ps1 -Script Tools/Blender/norton_assets.py -ScriptArguments @('head')`.

## Walker escape into mist

Full flashlight protection now commits the Walker to physically retreating, including after it exits the beam. It uses collision-checked navigation toward distant mist instead of resuming pursuit at the light's edge. Its silhouette gradually fades between 24 and 32 metres; after reaching 32 metres it deactivates and waits a random 10–15 seconds of active play. It returns at a clear, reachable point at least 30 metres from the current player, outside the camera view, then approaches through the mist. A shelter postpones its return. The scripted web attack and blackout escape retain ownership of the Walker, and point-blank attacks still bypass flashlight protection. MechanicsTest retains its direct light-response fixture.

## Source art and references

- [Creomoto's Car](https://opengameart.org/content/car-0), published by its creator under **CC0**. The original `.blend`, source/download URL and SHA-256 are retained in `ArtSource/Vendor/creomoto`. The game model is a scaled, damaged and charred adaptation.
- The head, casualty and gripping hands use the already retained **CC0 MakeHuman/MPFB anatomy, system clothing and Mindfront skin** described in `ThirdPartyNotices.md`. Original skin UVs, normal and roughness maps remain attached. The six continuous organic legs, 28-bone rig, five animation clips, viscera and shotgun geometry are authored in this project.
- [The Thing: Remastered — Nightdive Studios](https://nightdivestudios.com/the-thing-remastered/) explicitly describes scuttling head-spiders. This supplies the head-on-articulated-legs design reference. No models, textures, audio or animation files were extracted from that game.
- Wreck flames, drifting soot, embers, muzzle smoke and fireballs use an original Unity particle shader. Ten limited mono clips include original weapon/fire synthesis and distinct processed layers from the retained CC0 ghost voice. The added `head_score.wav` is an original seamless oscillator composition. `generate_norton_audio.py` records the exact reproducible processing.
