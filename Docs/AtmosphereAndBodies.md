# Night atmosphere and human bodies — 0.4.0

**Historical art pass:** the wildlife/fog described here remain. Version 0.5.0
replaces these procedural human models with licensed MakeHuman anatomy and skin.
Use [CombatAndCharacters.md](CombatAndCharacters.md) for the current bodies and
rebuild; `forest_bodies.py` is retained as an older authoring source only.

The expanded forest now has five additional original sounds generated by
`Tools/generate_night_audio.py`: a periodic 18-second stereo cricket bed and
mono owl, nightjar-style call, frog and leaf-rustle voices. These are synthesized
game sounds, not field recordings. Bird calls move between distant canopy
positions; frogs and leaves have their own quieter spatial sources. Indoor
volume/low-pass filtering muffles the wildlife. Proximity and the explicit
attack warning duck the ambience. The existing wolf and creature sounds remain.

`ForestAmbience` is driven by the ordered game simulation. New game/checkpoint
restore resets the seeded sequence. Zero simulation time advances neither the
event timers nor calls; the existing AudioListener pause suspends actual
playback in menus, the map and the journal.

Distance fog uses exponential-squared density 0.026, rising to 0.044 with Dread,
and a muted grey-blue horizon/sky. `ForestAtmosphere` controls 42 drifting
billboards (13–21m wide, 5–8m high) around the player, alongside 50 wider low
ground banks. Layered
noise, soft depth intersections, camera facing and a light-cone tint give the
air a rolling smoke-like appearance. Density eases inside buildings; airborne
wisps avoid building footprints. A near-camera fade keeps inspected objects
readable. `_AtmosphereTime` is simulation-controlled, so shader drift also
freezes on pause. Fog is visual and does not grant light protection. This is a
bounded real-time approximation, not a volumetric ray-marching renderer.

Four original Blender human casualties replace the old oval/tube proxies.
`ArtSource/Blender/forest_body_01.blend` through `forest_body_04.blend` contain
fused head/neck/facial anatomy, closed eyelids, ears, nose/lips, scalp hair,
hands with separate fingers and opposed thumbs, four dressed arm poses,
tailored jackets, pockets, stitching, zips, trouser folds and laced boots.
Leather satchels and folded documents stay connected to the same four story
pickups; the surveyor still grants the map.

Each corpse has approximately 65,000 / 24,700 / 8,400 triangles in three LODs.
Human skin reuses the retained original generated epidermis with a physically
consistent texture scale on these static models. Two new 2048px fabric/leather
PBR sets add surface detail. Close-light response preserves skin pigment,
dark hair and eye creases. Smooth hidden shadow envelopes replace the tiny
geometry's oversized spotlight shadow artifacts. The flashlight's visual
intensity was calibrated from 7 to 3; its protection geometry/rules are unchanged.
The 2048px additional-light shadow atlas reserves a 1024px flashlight map;
small point lights use 256px faces to avoid automatic global downscaling.
The figures are authored game characters, not scanned people or a completed
production character library.

Regenerate the human models alone with the Unity editor closed:

```powershell
& 'C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe' --background --python-exit-code 1 --python Tools/Blender/forest_bodies.py -- --project C:\dev\UntilTheLastLight
& 'C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe' --background --python-exit-code 1 --python Tools/Blender/export_assets.py -- --project C:\dev\UntilTheLastLight
python Tools/generate_audio.py
./Tools/Invoke-Unity.ps1 -Task Generate
./Tools/Invoke-Unity.ps1 -Task Capture
./Tools/Invoke-Unity.ps1 -Task Smoke
./Tools/Invoke-Unity.ps1 -Task Build
```

The full Blender and audio workflows include these builders. No new network
service or key is required. `15-body-1.png` through `18-body-4.png` and
`19-human-face.png` show the imported models in Unity; `02-service-trail.png`
and `05-dread-forest.png` show the denser forest atmosphere.
