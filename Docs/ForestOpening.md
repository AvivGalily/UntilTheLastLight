# Forest opening implementation

New Game now plays the 110-second first-person opening in `Assets/_Game/Generated/Scenes/ForestGame.unity`. Open that scene, enter Play Mode and choose **התחלת המסע / Begin the journey**. Continue loads the existing checkpoint without replaying the drive.

During the drive, use A/D, left/right arrows or the controller's horizontal left stick. W / controller right trigger accelerates the vehicle within a bounded twelve-metre lead; the elapsed scene/dialogue clock always advances at ordinary speed. The lead eases away at gate/impact landmarks, so the full conversation and 110-second scene do not skip or run faster. Move the mouse or right stick to look left/right, down at the driver's clothed torso/legs, or toward the rear seats. Horizontal turns reach ±175°, vertical turns ±65°, with a 1.35× opening look multiplier. Side views lean 7 cm toward the relevant window; rear views move into the centre aisle to clear the headrest. The camera stays inside the cabin at the driver's eye position; a small seated downward angle keeps the anatomical hands/wheel in view. The look/steer/accelerate hint remains for twelve seconds. Steering allows ±1.6 metres and turns the wheel up to 45°, while forward heading follows the route. Release returns toward the centre; gate assistance narrows the range to ±0.08 metres. Escape/controller Start pauses. After completing the opening once, hold Space/controller A for two seconds to skip. Both completion and skip initialize the original ranger-cabin state.

## Implementation and assets

`Assets/_Game/Scripts/Intro/` contains the configurable sequence, rail vehicle, wheel/forearm posing, dialogue, passenger posing, layered audio and transition bridge. `ForestIntroConfig.asset` contains timing and twelve distinct voice slots per language. Events and dialogue follow elapsed simulation time; pause stops sequence time and listener playback. Camera parenting and the disabled controller/hand state are restored on completion, skip or cancellation.

The main camera sits in the driver seat throughout. A separate 512×256 camera has no AudioListener and serves only the physical rear-view mirror. The original gameplay camera/listener/UI and save systems are reused. The environmental opening root is disabled after the blackout handoff. Ordinary AI, active play time and flashlight drain do not advance during the opening; autosave happens after gameplay control returns.

The retained Blender sources author the 4×4 interior/wheel, three seated CC0 MakeHuman passengers and a complete first-person seated driver. The passengers retain the later casualties' macro build, age and clothes, with distinct short02/short03/short04 hair and restrained nose/lip/jaw morphs. The driver has an anatomical torso, field jacket sleeves, trousers, articulated legs and boots. Its head/oral geometry is excluded from first-person rendering; the shoulders, elbows and wrists are weighted bones that follow the wheel through two-bone IK, with four curled fingers and an opposing thumb at each grip. The old equipment-hand meshes and stretched rigid forearms are no longer used in the car. UV-mapped dashboard, door panels, upholstery, ribbed floor mats and wheel use retained project colour/normal maps plus a dedicated procedural grain/weave PBR shader. Exact geometry/material records are in the asset manifest. Editable models/prefabs, road, forest LODs, mirror and supplied audio are included.

The opening's fixed fence sections are scaled from their ten-metre source width to 4.1 metres; they no longer overlap the six-metre entrance. Hinged leaves start at 90° open and only close after the scheduled crossing beat and the actual vehicle centre is at least six metres beyond the gate. The supplied gate sound starts once with that safe closing movement.

The opening uses **all twelve user-supplied English MP3 takes**, including `VO_Jack_L02.mp3`. The supplied Runway manifest identifies Tyler as Chad, Jack as Clint and Ethan as Brodie. Complete takes are decoded to mono 24 kHz / 16-bit PCM WAVs in `Assets/_Game/Audio/VO/English`, preserving the existing Unity GUIDs. No words are trimmed and no playback speed or pitch is changed. They are preloaded and play through the existing three seat-positioned sources, without a runtime network dependency. Ambience/gate and threat ease down while a voice plays, then recover smoothly. English audio also plays with Hebrew subtitles; an assigned Hebrew take overrides that fallback. Windows include at least 150 ms after each take. L06 starts at 44.6 s, Jack's final warning at 86.25 s and Tyler's at 89.6 s; Tyler's full 2.08-second recording ends at 91.68 s, before the 92-second blast. The scene remains 110 seconds long.

[Listen to the supplied dialogue audition](Audio/ForestOpening_Dialogue_Preview.wav). It shortens gaps and omits the driving/SFX mix. [Voice manifest](Audio/ForestOpening_VoiceManifest.json) retains spoken text, supplied voice/task IDs, start, duration, levels and source/output SHA-256. Original MP3s and the unchanged supplied manifest are retained in `ArtSource/Audio/Runway`. Previous Kokoro takes and their manifest are archived outside Unity in `ArtSource/Audio/LegacyIntro`.

The four supplied effects replace the previous core layers. `SFX_ForestVehicleAmbience` is the only supplied loop, with a 150 ms overlap giving its eight-second source a continuous 7.85-second loop, fading at 69–73 s. `SFX_ForestGate` plays once when the vehicle has safely cleared the gate and its leaves begin closing (about 66.1 s at ordinary speed). `SFX_CreatureApproach` plays once at 79.75 s before Ethan hears it. `SFX_VehicleExplosion` plays once at 92 s with its complete six-second tail. W changes vehicle motion rather than dialogue/audio playback speed. The files remain preloaded PCM in `Assets/_Game/Audio/Opening`; existing tinnitus, breathing and wake rustle supplement them. [Effect receipt](Audio/ForestOpening_SuppliedAudio.json) and [dry audition](Audio/ForestOpening_ThreatImpact_Preview.wav) retain provenance/measurements.

## Reproduction

1. Run `Tools/Run-BlenderStep.ps1 -Script Tools/Blender/forest_intro.py -LogName ForestIntroArt`, then `Tools/Run-BlenderStep.ps1 -Script Tools/Blender/intro_driver.py -LogName IntroDriverArt`. The driver uses the healthy seated Tyler source rig with its own first-person extraction/bind pose. Focused `export_assets.py --asset <id> --manifest-only` validates already exported models and updates the manifest without rewriting FBXs.
2. Run `python Tools/import_intro_audio.py --source ArtSource/Audio/Runway` with ffmpeg available (or install `imageio-ffmpeg`). This uses the retained supplied MP3s and manifest; it does not generate speech. `--source` may also name a directory containing the original downloaded files.
3. Run `Tools/Invoke-Unity.ps1 -Task Intro` to install/update the opening in the existing scene. This preserves configured timing and VO references. Full `Generate` also wires the opening after creating the existing world/navigation.
4. Run `Tools/Invoke-Unity.ps1 -Task IntroAudio` to import all sixteen supplied files, update VO timing and replace just the opening effects group. This focused installer preserves scene art, the vehicle and the three passenger voice sources.
5. Run `-Task IntroSmoke`, `-Task EditTests`, `-Task PlayTests`, `-Task Validate`, `-Task Smoke` and `-Task Build` as appropriate. Intro verification saves to a unique temporary directory and restores language/seen preferences. The existing forest smoke suite bypasses the opening deliberately; the dedicated intro suite covers the drive and transition.

`generate_intro_audio.py`, `generate_intro_voices.py` and `ArtSource/Audio/ForestIntroVoice.json` retain the previous procedural/Kokoro authoring approach for reference. Running the legacy VO generator replaces the supplied voice WAVs; use `import_intro_audio.py` and `IntroAudio` to restore the current supplied-audio version.

## Remaining production work

- Supplied English VO is complete for L01–L12. Hebrew recordings are optional production work; current Hebrew mode uses English audio with translated subtitles.
- The vehicle is a functional procedural art pass. Further authored body/interior detail, windshield condensation and dust polish remain; this is not claimed as a finished production vehicle.
- Hunters have distinct age/build, hair, nose/lip/jaw contours, clothing, iris maps and seated reactions. Bespoke facial acting, precise lip sync and recorded laughter remain unfinished.
- No new focus-blur effect or production cabin-breath recording was added. The existing cabin receives a low camera wake/rise, slow fade and retained breathing with a rustling sound substitute. Purpose-recorded cabin wood/ambience still needs an audio pass.
- The monster and source of the blast remain unseen. Reference-hardware frame-rate benchmarking and human comfort/audio playtesting are not complete.

Each wheel hand retains its own native left/right skeleton. Palm-normal signs account for anatomical reflection; mirrored radial/tangent wrist frames put both palms against the rim and thumbs inside its opening. Finger joints follow circular chords using their individual phalanx lengths, with independently opposed thumbs. `ArtSource/IntroDriverPose.json` records the palm frames and mirrored finger-joint error. The regression checks thumb side, wrist roll, knuckle contact and forward finger curl at neutral and both steering limits.

The passengers appear in three separate encounters after the blast: Tyler is the first map-bearing corpse (`corpse_0`), Jack is the suspended web victim, and Ethan is the eviscerated casualty beside the burning research-station car (`norton_casualty`). Their exact opening faces, hair, clothing and UVs are retained. The two extra bodies beside Tyler are removed. Map/axe, letter, item IDs and encounter progression remain connected to their existing interactions. Jack retains the hanging/overpowered/dying animation, bone-attached silk and feeding sequence. Ethan has an opened abdomen and continuous intestinal coils with cylindrical UVs, a generated capillary-rich surface map and wet material. [Encounter provenance](../ArtSource/EncounterCast.json) records the mapping; [tissue provenance](../ArtSource/Textures/IntestinalSerosa_Imagegen.json) records the generated artwork.

Gameplay footsteps and the walking/running shadow share a distance-driven gait phase. A step is emitted only after grounded movement advances a footfall; stopping or entering menus/the opening stops the current clip immediately. Running increases the cadence through real movement speed. The shadow has alternating planted and swinging legs and opposing arm movement instead of whole-body rocking. The native left flashlight and right axe/shotgun hands now retain weighted upper-arm, elbow, wrist and finger anatomy. Two-bone IK preserves segment lengths and equipment grips without stretching or shearing mesh vertices. The old equipment fill light is permanently disabled: holding a weapon cannot create a light when the flashlight is off. Muzzle flashes and environmental lamps/fire retain their own behavior.

Gameplay breathing is conditional rather than an idle loop. Actual sprint movement uses only `player_breath_hard`, with source volume capped at 0.05 (about 79% below the previous 0.24 cap). Stationary stamina recovery uses only the slower `player_breath_calm`, capped at 0.018 and fading with the remaining stamina deficit. Walking is silent even while recovering; reaching the displayed 100% (stamina at least 0.995), reset, menus and disable stop both sources. Wounds no longer enable breathing. Existing audio clips are reused; the three PlayMode breathing regressions check playback ownership, volumes, winded walking, falling recovery level, full-stamina silence, reset, pause-stop and disable. Full combat smoke checks the same transitions using the real player motor and assigned clips.

Rebuild the body with `player_presence.py -- body`, equipment with `first_person_arms.py`, Tyler with `arrival_cast.py`, and Jack/Ethan with `encounter_cast.py`. The equipment author reloads its saved rest rig before FBX export to refresh Blender's rebased-bone cache. Use focused `export_assets.py --asset <id> --manifest-only` for receipts, then `-Task PlayerRefinements`. `-Task PlayerSmoke` captures actual grips, look-down, recoil, reload, flashlight-off, grounded gait and the three encounter roles over separate player frames. Tests check grip-frame offsets, bone lengths, unchanged mesh data, matched cast materials, wet intestinal UV/material inputs and disabled player fill light.

Verification evidence is in `Docs/ForestOpeningVerification.md`; Unity captures include the drive, cast, windows, steering, open gate, driver body and anatomical grips in `Docs/Screenshots/72` through `87`.
